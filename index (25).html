The Sign-Off

Software is about to decide which applications get accepted: who gets a job interview, who gets a loan, or who gets an apartment. It scores every applicant from 0 to 100 and rejects everyone below a cutoff. You set the cutoff, see who it gets wrong, and put your name on the decision.

1Pick the decision you will sign

Pick one of three application screeners. Each one reads applications and gives every applicant a score from 0 to 100. Applicants at or above the cutoff are accepted. Everyone below it is rejected.

You are seeing something no real manager sees. This page knows which applicants would have worked out, including the ones the model turned away. A real organization never learns that about the people it rejects. The applicants here are simulated so you can count the mistakes.

2Ask the vendor

The company that built the model will answer three questions. Open the ones you want answered before you sign. Your record shows which ones you asked.

At this cutoff

The approval-rate check divides the lower group's approval rate by the higher one. United States employment regulators use 0.80 as a rule of thumb, called the four-fifths rule. A ratio under 0.80 gets a closer look. It is a screening test, not a verdict, and it comes from hiring. In the loan and rental scenarios it is a yardstick only.
